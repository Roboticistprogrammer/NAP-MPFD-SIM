Predicting grasp outcome from a short lift

Adapting Zimmer et al. (2019) to a UAV-mounted gripper: protocol, simulation tests, a 150-trial dataset and what it can and cannot predict

PX4 SITL · Gazebo Harmonic · x500 quadrotor with Armbot gripper · November 2026

Where the Zimmer-style small-lift protocol stands in simulation: what was built, the 150-trial dataset, what the models can and cannot predict, and the decisions that are open.

Research question

Can a drone's own flight data, recorded during a short standard lift, tell us whether a grasp will hold?

Input

Telemetry the real drone already has: thrust, torque, motors, IMU, attitude, vertical state, plus the commanded gripper state (Tier-1).

Output

y_success: the object survives the full lift.
y_stable: it survives a lateral shake.

Why it matters

The real gripper has open-loop servos and no force feedback. A prediction early in the lift could trigger abort and re-grasp before the payload is dropped.

The grasp quality is not observable directly on the real arm; the drone dynamics are the only sensor we have in the base setup.

The short version

What works

The payload is clearly visible in the drone data (26–210× the hover noise).

150 trials, balanced labels, every acceptance gate passed.

A failing grasp is detected with AUC 0.91 once the window reaches 1.0 s.

What does not (yet)

Early warning: AUC 0.51 / 0.64 / 0.74 at 0.4 / 0.6 / 0.8 s, a lead of only 0.2–0.4 s.

y_stable is not predictable from the small lift.

Six ways to buy lead time did not help.

What decides next

Is detection enough, or is a grasp-sensing input needed?

A real-drone check of the signal level.

The simulated testbed

  • PX4 SITL with Gazebo Harmonic; ground truth fed to EKF2 as emulated motion capture.
  • x500 with the Armbot arm and a force-controlled two-finger gripper.
  • One cylinder (r 25 mm, L 120 mm, 0.15 kg) on a table, held by two rails.
  • A: grasp at the centre, fails by sliding out. B: grasp 30 mm off-centre, fails by pivoting round the fingers.
  • E: empty-gripper control (not used for classification).

Labels, from simulator ground truth

y_success

The cylinder stays in the grasp through the small lift, full lift and hold.

Failure when it moves > 40 mm relative to the grasp point, or its axis tilts > 45°. A move > 10 mm counts as slip.

y_stable

Only for held grasps: the same rules, counted from the start of the lateral shake (±0.3 m along the cylinder axis).

Empty (not 0) when the grasp already failed.

  • Failure mode is also logged: slide (falls out) or tilt (pivots round the fingers).
  • Invalid trials (missed grasp, alignment timeout) are excluded.

What the model may see, and what it may not

Model inputs (21 channels, 50 Hz)

  • thrust and torque setpoints (3 + 3)
  • motor commands (4)
  • body rates (3), accelerometer (3)
  • roll, pitch (2)
  • height, vertical speed and acceleration (3)
  • static: gripper closed, commanded arm pose

Privileged: labels and analysis only

  • applied grip force: decides the outcome in the simulator
  • scenario (A or B): a proxy for it
  • cylinder displacement, tilt, pose
  • finger travel (sim-only Tier-2 ablation)

Leakage control: results are reported per scenario against that scenario's own majority class, so a model cannot score by recognising A versus B.

Source: config/channels.yaml
The hover thrust estimate was checked and is constant in this sim, so it was dropped from the inputs (21 channels instead of 22).

Finding 1: the drone does not follow the commanded lift

Position setpoint only

With velocity feed-forward

  • First the drone must take the cylinder's weight (thrust +3–4 %, no motion for ~1.3 s); PX4's position loop then lags by ~2 s.
  • Feed-forward: also send the velocity the profile needs. Standard PX4 offboard feature, flyable on the real drone.
Data: sim_data/20261016_164146_protocol (t0008), 20261017_004908_protocol (t0005)
Left: trial from the first pilot, 0.10 m / 1.5 s, position setpoint only. Right: trial from the final confirmation run, 0.15 m / 1.5 s with feed-forward. Shaded area is the 1.5 s small-lift window.

Shorter distance or longer time? Neither. Feed-forward.

Share of the commanded rise the drone had actually climbed at the end of the SL window (FF = velocity feed-forward).

  • Shorter distance: the lag is load transfer plus the slow position loop, so 6 cm is swallowed (0 %).
  • Longer time: 49 % at 4 s, and the window then contains the failures.
  • Feed-forward tracks 61–90 %. Chosen: 0.15 m / 1.5 s + FF (85 %).

5–6 trials per row, scenario A. Indicative, not statistics.

Finding 2: held and failed grasps look alike until the drop

  • Thrust rises up to ~6 % of hover (pick-up plus the climb), against 0.03–0.07 % hover noise.
  • Both groups follow the same curve until ~0.9 s; by 1.5 s held trials sit 1.7 points higher.
  • The curves separate only around the drop: the warning before it is the hard part.

Fix: one shake intensity per scenario

scenario Ascenario B
  • New config block shake.per_scenario: only the leg time differs (the ±0.3 m amplitude and the grip forces stay).
  • A needs a harsher shake than the 3.0 s legs of the first pilot: 0 of 9 held grasps failed it; at 2.0 s, 15 of 17 did.
  • Chosen: A 2.25 s, B 1.25 s. In the 150-trial batch each scenario has 38 % unstable among its held grasps.

Each point is a count, not a rate over 100 trials: labels are unstable / held grasps, bars the 95 % interval. Pilots have only 8–20 held grasps, so their intervals are wide; the batch (A 13/34) is the reliable point.

Detectability is the plan milestone M1. The ratios compare the payload step in the drone channels with the standard deviation of the 0.5 s hover before the small lift. Source: result.md sections 14, 15 and 16
Each attempt used the same SVM and cross-validation as the baselines. The extra PX4 signals are all logged on the real drone as well. The tracking-error hint was checked with a permutation test (labels shuffled within scenario, 150 permutations: null mean 0.49, sd 0.06), but about 20 group-and-window cells were tested, so one at p = 0.013 is what chance produces; it does not grow with the window and no single channel carries it.

What the real gripper can and cannot give

SimulatorReal Armbot (open-loop servos)
ActuationTwo force-controlled fingers, 0–35 mm each; the grip level is a force in newtonsHobby servo commanded by angle (closed at about 96°); the squeeze comes from servo stiffness and stall torque
FeedbackFinger position is exact ground truthNone: the logger records the commanded angle and grip_state only
Grip forceCommanded, so knownNot set, not measured

Finger travel

Needs a hardware change (potentiometer tap, bus servo, encoder), and the simulator says it adds no early warning.

Squeeze proxies

Servo current or load, a bus servo with load feedback, or a force pad: the only inputs that could carry the margin before slip.

Why not test it in sim

A simulated force sensor reads the commanded force, which already predicts the outcome within A (AUC 0.95): nearly circular.

Source: 07_grasp_experiment.md limitations; small-lift-plan-brief.md; result.md section 15
Hardware per the project brief: Holybro X500 V2 with the CODLAI/JSumo ArmBot (5 joints, Arduino Nano, open-loop servos). The actual servo model was not inspected; please check it. The hardware logger already records the commanded gripper angle at 50 Hz in the Tier-1 CSV.

Questions for discussion (1/2)

  1. Contribution: is detecting an impending drop about 0.2–0.4 s ahead enough, or must the thesis show earlier prediction? What lead is useful on the real system: how long do abort and re-grasp take?
  2. Metric: move from AUC at fixed window ends to an online detector scored by detection delay against false alarms?
  3. Sensing: is a grasp-sensing channel in scope (force pad, servo current or load, bus servo)? Model it in the simulator first, or go straight to hardware?
  4. Real knob: rebuild the sim gripper as a position-controlled, torque-limited servo so the varied quantity matches the real one?
The answers to the first two decide whether the current result is already a result.

Questions for discussion (2/2)

  1. Real validation: how many real flights are needed to claim transfer? Start with about 20 that only measure the payload step against the real hover noise?
  2. Sim-only result: acceptable for this chapter if the real flights only confirm the signal level?
  3. Data: spend about 6 h on a fresh, held-out batch to test the one weak hint, or scale to 600 trials for the planned models?
  4. Models and y_stable: keep the CNN-LSTM in scope (the LSTM equals the SVM today)? Keep y_stable as a second task (unpredictable so far) or redefine it?
  5. Physical choices: may the payload, friction or pads change under the “two scenarios, many repetitions” decision, if that gives slower, creep-like failures?
Question 7 asks for permission to treat the tracking-error hint as a pre-stated hypothesis on new data.
